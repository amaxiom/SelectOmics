# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to each other. Discuss the work, not the person doing it.

## What is expected

This project exists so that people can argue productively about evidence. That
depends on a few things being reliably true of everyone taking part:

- **Criticise the claim, not the claimant.** "This result does not follow from
  that benchmark" is the kind of comment this project is for. "You clearly do
  not understand statistics" is not.
- **Assume the other person is trying.** A confused bug report usually means the
  documentation failed, not that the reporter is careless.
- **Take correction plainly.** Being wrong in public is the ordinary cost of
  working in the open. Nobody here is expected to pretend otherwise, in either
  direction.
- **Leave people's characteristics out of it.** Age, disability, body size,
  ethnicity, gender identity and expression, nationality, personal appearance,
  race, religion, sexual identity and orientation, level of experience and
  level of education have no bearing on whether a patch is correct.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment in any form, including sexualised language or attention, insults,
demeaning or derogatory comments, deliberate misgendering, sustained disruption
of discussions, and publishing anyone's private information without their
permission. Threats, of any kind, to anyone.

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code comments and
anywhere else the project is represented.

## Reporting

Report anything that troubles you to **amaxiomatic@gmail.com**. Reports are
read by the maintainer and are not shared further without your agreement,
except where the law requires it. You do not need to be certain that something
counts as a violation before reporting it.

If your report concerns the maintainer, say so, and say what you would find an
acceptable resolution.

## What happens then

There is no committee here and no process theatre. The maintainer will read the
report, ask questions if the facts are unclear, and respond in proportion: a
private word, a request to edit or withdraw a comment, a warning, or a block
from the repository. Serious or repeated conduct goes straight to a block.

You will be told what was decided. Where it is possible without exposing the
person who reported, the reasoning will be given too.

## Scope

This applies to everyone taking part in the project, including the maintainer,
in project spaces and when representing the project elsewhere.

## Attribution

The expectations above follow the spirit of the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/),
version 2.1, rewritten for a single-maintainer project so that it describes
what will actually happen rather than a process that does not exist here.
